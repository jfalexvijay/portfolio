INDEPENDENTLY DEVELOPED

FareDesk.

A practical invoice creation and management app for small businesses across Apple platforms. Both the iOS and macOS versions have been submitted to App Store Connect and are awaiting review. Built around local-first workflows, configurable business details, tax calculations, invoice status tracking, and PDF output.

SwiftSwiftUISwiftDataClean ArchitectureMVVMCoordinatorSwift TestingXCUITest

CASE STUDY

From multi-step forms
to a repeatable release process.

The engineering challenge

Coordinating a multi-step invoice form, maintaining consistent invoice data, generating a useful PDF, and connecting development work to an automated release workflow.

Why this architecture

As a solo developer with a long-term product roadmap, I chose Clean Architecture with repositories and use cases, MVVM, and a Coordinator for navigation. The goal is to keep the app testable, maintainable, and reusable where it provides real value as the product grows.

Outcome demonstrated

Improved UI-test workflows and configured automation across GitHub Actions and Xcode Cloud. The captured Xcode report shows 87.3% overall code coverage for the displayed build.

04 / HOW I ENGINEER

Build for correctness,
and for the person using it.

My decisions are guided by maintainability, confidence in behaviour, and the consequences a defect can have for a small business owner.

01 / ARCHITECTURE

Design for a growing product—even as a solo developer.

I chose Clean Architecture, MVVM, and Coordinator-based navigation because I am developing FareDesk independently and have a larger roadmap in mind. Clear boundaries help me test business rules, maintain features, and reuse components where appropriate without making every part of the app depend on every other part.

02 / TESTING

Test outcomes, not just lines executed.

My tests check whether expected text and UI elements appear, whether calculations produce the correct results with sample data, and whether navigation behaves as intended. I maintain predefined test models to exercise different available scenarios. Unit tests and UI tests serve different purposes, and code coverage is only one signal of quality.

03 / USER EXPERIENCE

Think about how a real business owner experiences failure.

I try to consider how a user would feel if a workflow failed, information looked wrong, or an unexpected state appeared. Since invoices support real business activity, I aim to make the app understandable and predictable, and to validate important calculations and flows instead of focusing only on the happy path.

04 / RESPONSIBLE ITERATION

Ship the first release, then close the data-protection gaps.

I prioritized getting the initial release ready while identifying backup and recovery as near-term work. I recognize that invoice records matter to users; I plan to add a dependable backup and recovery approach as soon as possible, and validate restoration and data integrity rather than treating backup as a checkbox.
